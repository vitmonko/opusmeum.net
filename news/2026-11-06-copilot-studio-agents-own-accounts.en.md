Microsoft's October 2026 update to Copilot Studio, published on 4 November, changes what an agent is. Until now, most agents waited for someone to ask them something. The new release moves them closer to digital colleagues that work in the background.

## What is new

- **Agents with their own accounts.** Agents can use their own approved credentials and scoped permissions, be @-mentioned in Teams, Outlook and Office files, remember a business process, and start work from events or schedules. Agent accounts are in private preview through Microsoft's Frontier program.
- **Work IQ is generally available in Copilot Studio.** Agents get context from across Microsoft 365 and business apps: emails, meetings, files and who works with whom.
- **Agents that work with other agents.** Support for the open Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol lets an agent hand tasks to other agents, including ones built outside Copilot Studio.
- **Better workflows.** Workflows can now call Copilot directly, pull structured data from documents, emails and images with a new Extract node, and be edited by several authors at once. All three are generally available.
- **Cost control.** Makers see Copilot Credit usage while they build and test, with reporting per workflow run. A new preview in the Microsoft 365 admin center adds spending limits per tenant, group, user or environment, with alerts.

## Why it matters

An agent with its own account and a schedule is no longer a chatbot. It is closer to a new employee: it needs a defined role, only the permissions the job requires, and someone responsible for its work.

The good news is that Microsoft is shipping the controls alongside the capabilities. Scoped permissions, spending limits and usage reporting mean you can start small and measure the results.

## How to prepare

1. **Pick one process, not ten.** Good first candidates are recurring and rule-based, like weekly supplier follow-ups or monthly report preparation.
2. **Give every agent an owner.** Someone in the business decides what the agent may do and checks its results.
3. **Scope access before you build.** Decide which sites, mailboxes and systems the agent needs, and nothing more.
4. **Set a budget.** Use Copilot Credit spending limits from day one, so a busy agent cannot surprise you on the invoice.

Agent accounts are still in preview, so now is a good time to pilot them with a small group and set up governance before they become broadly available.
