---
title: Why Copilot governance comes before Copilot rollout
summary: Copilot works with the permissions you already have. Here is what to fix before rollout, so it does not surface content nobody meant to share.
---
Microsoft 365 Copilot is only as safe as the permissions behind it. Copilot does not get new access to your data: it works with what each person can already open in SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams and Exchange. That sounds reassuring until you remember how much content in a typical tenant is shared more widely than anyone intended.

Before Copilot, an overshared folder was hard to stumble on. With Copilot, a simple question like "summarize this year's salary review" can surface it in seconds.

## Five things to settle before rollout

1. **Find and fix oversharing.** Review sites and libraries shared with "Everyone" or the whole organization, broken permission inheritance and old guest access. Start with HR, finance and management content.
2. **Label sensitive information.** Microsoft Purview sensitivity labels and data loss prevention policies let you mark confidential content and control how it can be used, including by Copilot.
3. **Decide who builds agents.** Copilot Studio makes it easy for anyone to build an agent. Use Power Platform environments and data policies so agents are built in the right place and only connect to approved data.
4. **Write a short usage policy.** People need to know what Copilot is for, what it should not be used for, and that they remain responsible for checking its output.
5. **Measure adoption.** Track which teams use Copilot and for what, so licenses go to the people who get value from them.

## Governance makes Copilot more useful, not less

Good governance is not about blocking Copilot. Clean permissions and well-organized content mean better answers, fewer surprises and faster trust from employees and management.
