Microsoft opened Ignite 2026 in San Francisco on 17 November with a set of changes to the Microsoft 365 Copilot app. The direction is clear: Copilot becomes the place where people write, calculate and present, and where agents keep working in the background. Some of it is generally available today. Much of it is still limited to the Frontier program or private preview.

## What is new

- **Home is generally available.** Home is the new starting point in the Copilot app, where Chat and Cowork come together. Chat can recommend moving a task to Cowork when it needs multistep work.
- **Office in Copilot is generally available.** Word, Excel and PowerPoint capabilities run inside the Copilot app. Files save to OneDrive and keep Microsoft 365 permissions, sensitivity labels and compliance controls. Microsoft says it is included in the Copilot subscription license.
- **Autopilot is rolling out to Frontier customers.** Autopilot is a personal, always-on agent that lives in your tenant with its own identity, memory and tools. Each one gets a Microsoft Entra Agent ID and a named manager, IT can scope or block its access, and its actions are logged under its own name.
- **Workspaces is available in Frontier.** A shared space where people and agents work towards a common goal with shared context. It evolves from Copilot Notebooks.
- **Today is in private preview.** A command center inside Home across mail, calendar, Teams threads, meetings and tasks, in the Copilot app and Outlook.
- **Copilot Code and Copilot Managed Runtime.** Copilot Code becomes generally available "in the coming weeks". The Copilot Managed Runtime, which hosts the apps people build in your tenant, is generally available now.
- **Spending limits in Cowork.** Teams can set a spending limit for Cowork, and Agent 365 Cost Management gives IT a central view of what drives spend.

## Why it matters

Office in Copilot changes how people will open and create documents, so training and support material written for the separate Office apps will start to feel dated. Autopilot and Copilot Code are a bigger shift: they let employees create agents and apps that keep running. Microsoft has built identity, logging and spend controls into both, but those controls only help if someone in IT owns them.

## What to do now

1. **Separate GA from Frontier.** Plan training for Home and Office in Copilot now. Treat Autopilot, Workspaces and Today as pilots.
2. **Decide on Frontier.** If you join, pick a small group and a named IT owner for Autopilot agents.
3. **Set spending limits before wide use of Cowork.** Agree who approves budgets and who receives alerts.
4. **Prepare for Copilot Code.** Decide who may build and publish apps, and where they will be reviewed, before it reaches general availability.
5. **Check governance basics first.** Permissions and labels matter even more when Office files live in Copilot. Our post on [Copilot governance before rollout](/news/copilot-governance/) lists the essentials.
