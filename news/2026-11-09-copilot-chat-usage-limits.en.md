On 3 November 2026, Microsoft told admins in the Message center (MC1536158 and MC1536163) that it is applying fair use limits to Copilot Chat features for users without a Microsoft 365 Copilot license in commercial tenants. According to Microsoft's Partner Center announcement of 4 November, the rollout began on 2 November for organizations with 2,000 or fewer seats and begins on 9 November for larger organizations.

## What is new

- **Limits for unlicensed users.** The limits apply in Copilot Chat on web, desktop and mobile. Image generation already had limits, and Microsoft is extending the approach to more Chat features. It says limits may cover more capabilities over time.
- **Limits change during the day.** When a feature is unavailable, users see a notice in the product and can try again later. Responses already in progress are not interrupted.
- **No numbers and no admin controls.** Microsoft does not publish specific numeric limits. Admins cannot view or adjust them, and no admin action is required.
- **Not an error.** Microsoft states that reaching a limit is not an error or a product malfunction.

## Why it matters

Many organizations rolled out free Copilot Chat to everyone and bought Microsoft 365 Copilot licenses for a small group. The people who use free Chat most will now be the first to hit limits, at times nobody can predict. Without preparation, those cases arrive at the help desk as "Copilot is broken".

Because the limits are not published, you cannot plan capacity around them. You can plan who needs a license.

## What to do now

1. **Brief the help desk.** Explain that some Copilot Chat features are limited for unlicensed users and that a limit message is expected behavior.
2. **Find your heavy unlicensed users.** Use the usage reports in the Microsoft 365 admin center, including the [agents usage report](/news/copilot-governance-controls/) that separates licensed and unlicensed users.
3. **Decide how license requests work.** Review self-service license requests in the Microsoft 365 admin center, so people who hit limits have a clear path that you control.
4. **Tell users what to expect.** A short note saying that limits exist and that work in progress is not lost prevents frustration.
5. **Revisit your license business case.** Teams that depend on Copilot Chat daily are good candidates for a full Microsoft 365 Copilot license.
